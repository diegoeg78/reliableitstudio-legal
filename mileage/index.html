Summary

MileReal keeps your trips, routes, photos and expenses on your phone. The developer has no server and no user accounts, shows no ads, and does not receive your trips, routes, photos or expenses. A few features contact other companies' servers, listed below under "What leaves your phone".

What the app stores on your phone

  • Trips: start and end odometer readings, dates and times, the purpose and notes you type, distances, and whether the odometer and GPS distances matched.
  • Routes: GPS points recorded while a trip is running.
  • Photos: the odometer photos you take for each trip, and optional receipt photos.
  • Expenses: toll and parking amounts, with an optional note and receipt photo.
  • Settings: country, distance unit, language, currency symbol, your own IRS-rate entries and reminder choice. The country is read once from your SIM card (or, without a SIM, from your phone's region setting) to choose the starting currency and distance unit and whether the US-only IRS features appear. It is never sent anywhere, and you can change it in Settings.
  • Subscription bookkeeping: how many trips you have started (for the 10 free trips) and Google Play's last answers about your subscription and your lifetime purchase.

This data stays in the app's private storage. It is excluded from Google's cloud backup and from device-to-device transfer. Uninstalling the app deletes it.

Location

The app uses your precise location only while you are recording a trip, from when you tap Start trip until you end the trip. A notification is shown the whole time. The app does not request background location access and does not record your location when no trip is open. The recorded route is saved only on your phone. (Map images of the area you are viewing are downloaded from the internet; see "Maps" below.)

Camera

The camera is used only when you photograph an odometer or a receipt. The odometer number is read on your phone by Google's ML Kit text recognition. The photos are not uploaded anywhere by the app.

What leaves your phone

  1. Maps (OpenStreetMap). To draw the map on the recording screen and on a trip's route, the app downloads map images ("tiles") from the OpenStreetMap Foundation's tile servers. Each request reveals your IP address and which map area is being shown, which is close to where you are or where you drove. Your route, trips and photos themselves are not sent. The OpenStreetMap Foundation handles these requests under its own privacy policy.
  2. Text recognition diagnostics (Google ML Kit). Reading the odometer happens on the phone; your photos and the numbers read from them are not sent. Google's ML Kit library sends Google technical diagnostics and usage data: device information (such as manufacturer, model and Android version), the app's package name and version, a per-installation identifier, performance and error information, and settings such as image size. Google uses this for diagnostics and usage analytics, sends it over an encrypted connection and does not transfer it to third parties (Google's ML Kit data disclosure).
  3. Subscription (Google Play). Payments are made and managed through Google Play under Google's terms and privacy policy. The developer does not receive your payment details. The app asks Google Play on your phone whether your subscription is active or your lifetime purchase is owned.
  4. IRS mileage rates (United States only). About once a month, the app reads the public IRS "Standard mileage rates" web page (irs.gov) to see whether a new rate was published. Like any web request, this reveals your IP address to the IRS website; nothing about you or your trips is sent. You can turn this off in Settings > IRS mileage rate.

The app does not sell any data, does not share data for advertising and does not use the advertising ID.

Subscription

The first 10 trips you start are free (once per installation, not every month). Starting more trips requires a monthly or yearly subscription, or a lifetime purchase, billed by Google Play at the price shown in the app before you buy. Subscriptions renew automatically, every month or every year, until you cancel. You can cancel at any time in Google Play (Play Store > Payments & subscriptions > Subscriptions, or in the app: Settings > Manage subscription); you keep access until the end of the period you paid for. The lifetime purchase is paid once and never renews. Refunds are handled by Google Play under its refund policy. Without a subscription or lifetime purchase you can still view, edit, export, back up and delete everything you recorded, and a trip that is already recording is never stopped.

Files you create

You can export trips as CSV or PDF and make backup files. These are created only when you ask, and saved or shared where you choose (for example your Downloads folder, Google Drive or an email). Once you share a file, the app or service you share it with handles it. A backup file is not password protected and contains your routes and photos: keep it somewhere safe.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your data is kept on your phone until you delete it. You can:

  • delete a single trip, expense or photo in the app;
  • delete photos by tax year in Settings > Photo storage;
  • replace everything with a backup in Settings > Backup & restore;
  • delete all app data in Android's settings (Apps > MileReal > Storage > Clear storage), or by uninstalling the app.

The developer holds no copy of your trips, routes, photos or expenses, so there is nothing on a developer server to access or delete. Data received by Google or the OpenStreetMap Foundation as described above is kept and deleted under their own policies.

Security

Your records are kept in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read. The internet requests described above use encrypted (https) connections. Backups and exports are as safe as the place you store them.

Not tax advice

IRS deduction figures (United States only) are estimates based on the published standard mileage rates and your own odometer readings. MileReal is not affiliated with, endorsed by or sponsored by the Internal Revenue Service. Ask a tax professional what applies to you.

Children

The app is a business tool for adults and is not directed at children under 13.

Aproximadamente una vez al mes, la app consulta la página pública de tarifas estándar de millaje del IRS (irs.gov) para ver si se publicó una tarifa nueva.
